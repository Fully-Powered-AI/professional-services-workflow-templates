# Engagement staffing plan

When several engagements want the same people, the resource lead sees the collision before kickoff: role needs against free days, leave and competing assignments, week by week, with two or three staffing options. The resource lead and partners choose.

*Workflow template · Professional services · Project and delivery · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/engagement-staffing-plan/)*

## The outcome

The clash between engagements shows up on a page weeks ahead, not in a corridor conversation in the week the work starts. For each role and qualification the page sets the days the committed engagements need beside the days the qualified people actually have free after leave, their current assignments and their review commitments. Where the two do not meet, it names the weeks, the days short and the engagements and bookings that are competing for the same people.

Tentative wins are kept apart. The page first shows what committed work alone requires, then what changes if the tentative engagement is also won, so nobody staffs a hope as though it were a contract.

Then come two or three options, each built only from levers the firm has stated: a start that may move within a stated range, or an assignment its partner has said can slide. Every option shows the shortfall it leaves and what it touches. The AI assistant assigns nobody and recommends nothing unless asked. The resource lead and the engagement partners choose assignments and agree any schedule change.

## Who starts the work, and when

The resource lead, the operations manager or a managing partner starts it when a new engagement is confirmed, when a tentative one gets close, or at the regular staffing review, ahead of the weeks in question. It runs again when leave is approved, a start date moves or an assignment changes. The resource lead reads it the same day and takes the options to the partners involved.

## What they supply

- **People:** each person's role, qualifications and standard days a week.
- **Leave:** each person's approved absences, with dates.
- **Current assignments:** who is booked on what, for which weeks and how many days a week, including review time, and whether the engagement's partner has said it can move.
- **Demand:** each engagement's open needs by role and qualification, with start, end, days a week, whether it is committed or tentative, the range in which its start may move, and the approved budget in days.
- **Settings:** the planning window, the days the office is closed and any notes, such as a partner's agreement to let a booking slide.

## What comes out

A plan in a fixed order: committed capacity by role and week, the collision named, the same comparison with tentative work added, two or three options with the shortfall each leaves, the input flags, the people not counted and why, and the decisions only the resource lead and partners can make. Any section with nothing to report says so. If a file's headings do not match the expected ones, nothing is calculated until the person says which column is which.

## What has to change for your company

Decide your roles and qualifications and keep the names identical across files. Decide who may cover which role, because as written a person fills only a line for their own role. Set the unit of time: the workflow template counts Monday to Friday weeks and days. State which bookings may move and who has agreed. Choose the ceiling for a part-time person's days. Decide how much of a week a partner has for review. It does not set hiring or headcount, which is [Headcount plan](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/headcount-plan/), and it does not score a new proposal, which is [Project intake and capacity review](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/project-intake-and-capacity-review/). It makes no judgment of any person's performance and ignores ratings or pay if they are supplied.
