# Engagement margin review

Midway or at close, the partner and finance lead get one worksheet for an engagement: recorded time, agreed fee, billed to date and supplied costs by workstream, the gaps that limit it, and the questions the team must answer. No cost rate is invented.

*Workflow template · Professional services · Finance and accounting · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/engagement-margin-review/)*

## The outcome

The engagement partner and the finance lead sit down with the same page. For each workstream it shows the fee that was agreed, what has been billed, the hours that were budgeted and the hours recorded, and the costs of delivery the firm has supplied: its own cost rates for the time, expenses and subcontractors. Where hours or costs are running past the plan, or where effort is well ahead of billing, the page says so and asks the engagement manager the question that explains it.

The page is just as clear about what it cannot see. Hours still awaiting approval, roles with no cost rate, subcontractor costs not yet invoiced and work recorded outside the fee schedule are each listed with their effect, so nobody mistakes a partial figure for a finished margin. The partner and the finance lead check every figure, and they alone decide whether to change staffing or take any action on the fee.

## Who starts the work, and when

The finance analyst or the engagement manager runs it when the timesheets and costs for an engagement are ready for a review: at the midpoint of a longer engagement, or at close before the final invoice and the retrospective. The partner and the finance lead review the same week. It is run on request, for one engagement at a time.

## What they supply

- **The time export** for the engagement: workstream, role, hours and approval status.
- **The fee schedule:** each workstream with its budgeted hours and agreed fee, and the total agreed fee.
- **The firm's cost rates** by role. The workflow template never supplies one.
- **The billing list:** each invoice by workstream, fee or recharge, with its amount.
- **The delivery costs:** expenses and subcontractor costs by workstream, invoiced or not yet invoiced.
- **At the midpoint, the engagement manager's remaining hours** by workstream.

## What comes out

One worksheet in a fixed order: the picture for the whole engagement, a tie-out, an entry for each workstream with three tests marked fired or not fired, the gaps that limit the view, numbered questions each addressed to a role, and the decisions that belong to the partner and the finance lead, each with its facts. If the fee schedule does not add up to the agreed fee, or a file does not have the expected columns, nothing is drafted until that is put right.

The worksheet recommends no staffing change and no fee action, sets no fee, writes nothing off and changes no record.

## What has to change for your company

Decide which fee bases you will review (fixed fee, capped fee or budgeted time and materials) and how the fee is split across workstreams. Agree who supplies the cost rates, how often they are refreshed and which roles they cover. Set the three tests: how far projected hours may pass the budget, how far effort may run ahead of billing, and what share of a workstream's fee its costs may reach. Decide when an engagement is reviewed, which decisions the partner takes alone and which go to the finance lead as well, and what counts as approved time in your timesheet system.
